# Q30. What Is Replication?

**Database replication** is the process of maintaining copies of database data on multiple database servers, called **replicas**, and keeping those copies synchronized according to the replication mechanism.

A common setup is:

```text
                    Application
                         ↓
                      Primary
                    ↙         ↘
                   ↓           ↓
             Replica 1     Replica 2
                Reads         Reads
```

Typically:

* **Primary** → handles writes
* **Replicas** → handle appropriate read workloads

The exact architecture depends on the database system.

---

# How Does Replication Work?

Suppose the application executes:

```sql
UPDATE accounts
SET balance = balance - 1000
WHERE account_id = 101;
```

The primary records the change, and the database's replication mechanism propagates that change to the replicas.

Conceptually:

```text
Write
  ↓
Primary
  ↓
Replication Mechanism
  ├──→ Replica 1
  └──→ Replica 2
```

The exact mechanism varies by database system. Some systems replicate using transaction logs, write-ahead logs, binary logs, or other engine-specific mechanisms.

---

# Synchronous vs Asynchronous Replication

One important distinction is **when the primary considers a write sufficiently replicated**.

## 1. Synchronous Replication

The primary waits for confirmation from the required replica(s) before considering the operation complete.

```text
Primary
   ↓
Send Change
   ↓
Replica Confirms
   ↓
Commit / Acknowledgement
```

### Benefit

* Stronger guarantees about replica freshness
* Depending on the configuration, committed data can be guaranteed to exist on additional nodes

### Trade-offs

* Can increase write latency
* Availability can become more dependent on replica/network conditions
* A slow or unavailable required replica may affect writes

---

## 2. Asynchronous Replication

The primary does not wait for replicas to confirm every change before completing the write.

```text
Primary → Commit
    ↓
Replica catches up
```

### Benefit

* Lower write latency
* More tolerance of temporary replica/network delays

### Trade-off

A replica can temporarily be behind the primary.

This creates **replication lag**.

---

# Example of Replication Lag

Suppose:

```text
Primary:
Order #101 created
      ↓
Replica:
Still catching up...
```

The application creates an order and immediately performs a read from a replica.

If the replica has not received the change yet:

```text
Write → Primary
Read  → Replica

Result → Order #101 not found
```

This is an example of **stale replica data**.

Applications may therefore use strategies such as:

* Reading recent/critical data from the primary
* Routing reads appropriately
* Using database/application mechanisms that provide suitable read-after-write behavior

The exact solution depends on the database and application architecture.

---

# Why Is Replication Useful?

## 1. Read Scaling

Multiple replicas can serve read traffic.

```text
             Application
            /     |     \
           ↓      ↓      ↓
       Replica1 Replica2 Replica3
```

This can reduce the read workload on the primary.

---

## 2. High Availability

If the primary fails, a replica may be **promoted to primary**, depending on the database architecture and failover configuration.

```text
Normal:

Primary
  ↓
Replica


After Failure:

Primary ❌

Replica
   ↓
New Primary
```

Replication alone does not guarantee automatic failover; that usually requires additional monitoring, orchestration, or database-specific high-availability mechanisms.

---

## 3. Disaster Recovery

Replicas provide additional copies of data and can be part of a disaster-recovery architecture.

However:

> **Replication alone is not a complete backup strategy.**

---

## 4. Workload Separation

Reporting or read-heavy workloads can sometimes be directed to replicas instead of the primary.

```text
                 Primary
                ↙       ↘
          Transactions  Replica
                            ↓
                       Reporting
```

This helps reduce competition between transactional and reporting workloads.

---

# Replication vs Backup

Replication and backup are **not the same thing**.

### Replication

```text
Primary
   ↓
Replica
```

The replica is generally intended to remain relatively current.

### Backup

```text
Database
   ↓
Backup
   ↓
Point-in-time recovery
```

Backups are designed to recover from situations such as:

* Accidental deletion
* Data corruption
* Incorrect updates
* Application bugs
* Certain disaster scenarios

### Important Example

Suppose someone accidentally runs:

```sql
DELETE FROM orders;
```

If that deletion is replicated immediately:

```text
Primary
  ↓
DELETE replicated
  ↓
Replica
```

The replica may also contain the deletion.

Therefore:

> **Replication should not be treated as a replacement for backups.**

Point-in-time recovery and independent backups can provide protection against logical mistakes that replication may faithfully reproduce.

---

# Replication Lag

With asynchronous replication, replicas may temporarily fall behind the primary.

```text
Primary:
Order #101 created
Order #102 created
Order #103 created
        ↓
        ↓
Replica:
Order #101 received
        ↓
   Still catching up...
```

The delay is called **replication lag** or **replica lag**.

Lag can be influenced by factors such as:

* Network delays
* Replica workload
* Storage I/O
* Large write volumes
* Long-running operations
* Replication mechanism

---

# Replication and Read-After-Write

Consider an application where a user:

1. Creates an order.
2. Immediately opens the order page.

If the write goes to the primary but the next read goes to a lagging replica:

```text
Create Order
     ↓
Primary
     ↓
Read immediately
     ↓
Replica
     ↓
Order not yet visible
```

The application may appear inconsistent from the user's perspective.

Possible solutions include:

```text
Recent/critical read
       ↓
    Primary

Normal read
       ↓
    Replica
```

or using database-specific mechanisms for suitable read-after-write guarantees.

# Simple Replication Architecture

```text
                     Application
                    /           \
                   ↓             ↓
                Writes          Reads
                   ↓             ↓
                Primary      Read Router
                              ↙     ↘
                             ↓       ↓
                         Replica 1 Replica 2
```

This architecture can provide:

* Write handling through the primary
* Read scaling through replicas
* Additional copies of data
* Potential failover options

---

# Important Interview Point

Replication is not simply:

> "Copy the database once."

It is a **continuous mechanism for maintaining additional database copies** according to a specific replication model.

Important concepts include:

* Synchronous vs asynchronous replication
* Replication lag
* Read consistency
* Failover
* High availability
* Disaster recovery
* Operational complexity

---

# Common Interview Mistakes

### ❌ Mistake 1: "Replication is the same as backup."

False.

Replication keeps additional copies relatively current; backups are designed for recovery and can provide point-in-time protection.

### ❌ Mistake 2: "Replicas always have exactly the same data as the primary."

Not necessarily.

With asynchronous replication, replicas can temporarily lag behind.

### ❌ Mistake 3: "Read replicas can handle every type of read safely."

Not always.

Reads that require the latest committed data may need the primary or an appropriate consistency mechanism.

### ❌ Mistake 4: "Replication automatically provides failover."

Not necessarily.

Failover may require additional monitoring, orchestration, or database-specific high-availability mechanisms.

### ❌ Mistake 5: "Replication only improves performance."

Replication can also support:

* High availability
* Disaster recovery
* Workload separation

But it also introduces consistency and operational considerations.

---

## Interview Takeaway

> **Replication is the process of maintaining synchronized copies of database data across multiple servers. It is commonly used for read scaling, high availability, disaster recovery, and workload separation, but introduces considerations such as replication lag, consistency, failover, and additional operational complexity.**
