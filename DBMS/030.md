30.What is replication?
Database replication is the process of maintaining copies of database data on multiple database servers (replicas) and keeping those copies synchronized according to the replication mechanism.

A common setup is:

                 Application
                     ↓
                  Primary
                 /       \
                ↓         ↓
          Replica 1   Replica 2
             Reads       Reads

The primary generally handles writes, while replicas can serve appropriate read workloads.

How replication works

Suppose the application executes:

UPDATE accounts
SET balance = balance - 1000
WHERE account_id = 101;

The primary records the change and replication mechanisms propagate that change to replicas.

Conceptually:

Write
  ↓
Primary
  ↓
Replication mechanism
  ↓
Replica 1
Replica 2

The exact mechanism varies by database system.

Synchronous vs asynchronous replication
Synchronous replication

The primary waits for confirmation from the required replica(s) before considering the operation complete.

Primary
   ↓
Replica confirms
   ↓
Commit/acknowledgement

Benefit: Stronger guarantees about replica freshness.

Trade-off: Can increase write latency and make availability more dependent on replica/network conditions.

Asynchronous replication

The primary does not wait for replicas to confirm every change.

Primary → Commit
    ↓
Replica catches up

Benefit: Lower write latency and better tolerance of replica/network delays.

Trade-off: A replica can temporarily be behind the primary, creating replication lag.

Why is replication useful?

Read scaling

Multiple replicas can serve read traffic.

High availability

A replica may be promoted if the primary fails, depending on the database architecture and failover setup.

Disaster recovery

Replicas can provide additional copies of data, although replication alone is not a complete backup strategy.

Workload separation

Reporting or read-heavy workloads can sometimes be directed to replicas instead of the primary.

Replication vs backup

They are not the same.

Replication:

Primary → Replica

The replica is intended to stay relatively current.

Backup:

Database → Backup at a point in time

Backups are designed for recovery from situations such as accidental deletion or corruption.

For example, if an accidental DELETE is replicated immediately, the replica may also receive that deletion. Therefore, replication should not be treated as a replacement for backups.

Replication lag

With asynchronous replication:

Primary:  Order #101 created
             ↓
Replica:  still catching up...

A user might write data and immediately perform a read against a replica that hasn't received the change yet.

This is called replication lag or replica lag.

Applications therefore sometimes need strategies such as reading recent/critical data from the primary or using mechanisms that provide appropriate read-after-write behavior.

Interview takeaway

Replication is the process of maintaining synchronized copies of database data across multiple servers. It is commonly used for read scaling, high availability, and disaster recovery, but it introduces considerations such as replication lag, consistency, failover, and additional operational complexity.
