# Q15. How Do You Calculate Waiting Time and Turnaround Time?

**Waiting Time (WT)** and **Turnaround Time (TAT)** are two important metrics used to evaluate **CPU scheduling algorithms**.

They help us understand:

* How long a process takes to complete after arriving.
* How much time a process spends waiting for CPU service.
* How efficiently a scheduling algorithm handles processes.

The two most important formulas to remember are:

```text
Turnaround Time = Completion Time − Arrival Time

Waiting Time = Turnaround Time − Burst Time
```

---

# 1. Turnaround Time

## Definition

**Turnaround Time (TAT)** is the total time taken by a process from the time it **arrives in the system/ready queue** until the time it **completes**.

### Formula

$$
\boxed{\text{Turnaround Time} = \text{Completion Time} - \text{Arrival Time}}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{TAT = CT - AT}
$$

Where:

* **CT (Completion Time)** = time when the process finishes execution
* **AT (Arrival Time)** = time when the process enters the ready queue

### What does turnaround time include?

Turnaround time includes the complete time spent by the process in the system:

```text
Arrival
   ↓
Waiting for CPU
   ↓
Running on CPU
   ↓
Possibly waiting again
   ↓
Running again
   ↓
Completion
```

Therefore:

> **Turnaround Time = Waiting Time + CPU execution time + other delays**

For a simple single-CPU scheduling problem:

$$
TAT = WT + BT
$$

where **BT** is the total CPU burst time.

---

# 2. Waiting Time

## Definition

**Waiting Time (WT)** is the total time a process spends waiting in the **ready queue for CPU time**.

### Formula

$$
\boxed{\text{Waiting Time} = \text{Turnaround Time} - \text{Burst Time}}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{WT = TAT - BT}
$$

Since:

$$
TAT = CT - AT
$$

we can substitute:

$$
WT = (CT - AT) - BT
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{WT = CT - AT - BT}
$$

Where:

* **WT** = Waiting Time
* **TAT** = Turnaround Time
* **CT** = Completion Time
* **AT** = Arrival Time
* **BT** = CPU Burst Time

---

# Important Relationship Between TAT and WT

The relationship is:

$$
\boxed{TAT = WT + BT}
$$

Therefore:

```text
Turnaround Time
      │
      ├── Waiting Time
      │
      └── CPU Burst Time
```

Or:

```text
TAT = WT + BT
```

This relationship is extremely useful when solving CPU scheduling problems.

---

# Example

Suppose we have:

| Process | Arrival Time (AT) | Burst Time (BT) | Completion Time (CT) |
| ------- | ----------------: | --------------: | -------------------: |
| P1      |                 0 |               5 |                    5 |
| P2      |                 1 |               3 |                    8 |

Assume **FCFS scheduling**.

---

# Calculating P1

P1 arrives at time 0:

```text
P1:
AT = 0
BT = 5
CT = 5
```

## Turnaround Time

$$
TAT = CT - AT
$$

$$
TAT = 5 - 0
$$

$$
\boxed{TAT = 5}
$$

## Waiting Time

$$
WT = TAT - BT
$$

$$
WT = 5 - 5
$$

$$
\boxed{WT = 0}
$$

Therefore:

```text
P1:
Turnaround Time = 5
Waiting Time    = 0
```

This makes sense because P1 arrives at time 0 and immediately gets the CPU.

---

# Calculating P2

P2 arrives at time 1:

```text
P2:
AT = 1
BT = 3
CT = 8
```

P1 is already using the CPU from time 0 to 5.

Therefore, P2 has to wait from:

```text
Time 1 → Time 5
```

So its waiting time is:

$$
5 - 1 = 4
$$

Let's calculate it using the formula.

## Turnaround Time

$$
TAT = CT - AT
$$

$$
TAT = 8 - 1
$$

$$
\boxed{TAT = 7}
$$

## Waiting Time

$$
WT = TAT - BT
$$

$$
WT = 7 - 3
$$

$$
\boxed{WT = 4}
$$

Therefore:

```text
P2:
Turnaround Time = 7
Waiting Time    = 4
```

---

# Visualizing It Using a Gantt Chart

Assume FCFS scheduling:

```text
Time →    0         1              5          8
          |---------|--------------|----------|
              P1         waiting       P2
              └──────────┘              └─────┘
                CPU                      CPU
```

A clearer representation is:

```text
Time →    0              5              8
          |--------------|--------------|
               P1              P2
             running         running
```

P2 arrives at time 1:

```text
P2 arrives
    ↓
Time = 1
    │
    │ waits
    │
Time = 5
    ↓
P2 gets CPU
```

Therefore:

$$
WT_{P2} = 5 - 1 = 4
$$

P2 then executes for 3 units:

```text
5 → 8
```

So:

$$
CT_{P2} = 8
$$

and:

$$
TAT_{P2} = 8 - 1 = 7
$$

Therefore:

```text
P2:
Waiting Time    = 4
Turnaround Time = 7
```

---

# Why Is P2's Turnaround Time 7 Instead of 3?

This is an important interview point.

P2 needs only **3 CPU units**:

```text
Burst Time = 3
```

But it arrives at time 1 and completes at time 8.

Therefore:

```text
Time spent in system = 8 − 1 = 7
```

The extra 4 units are waiting time:

```text
7 total turnaround
│
├── 4 waiting
│
└── 3 CPU execution
```

Therefore:

$$
7 = 4 + 3
$$

or:

$$
TAT = WT + BT
$$

---

# General Method to Calculate WT and TAT

When solving a CPU scheduling problem, follow these steps.

## Step 1: Determine the Scheduling Order

First determine which process runs when.

For example:

```text
P1 → P2 → P3
```

This depends on the scheduling algorithm:

* FCFS
* SJF
* SRTF
* Round Robin
* Priority Scheduling
* etc.

---

## Step 2: Draw the Gantt Chart

For example:

```text
0       5       8       10
|-------|-------|--------|
   P1      P2       P3
```

The Gantt chart helps determine each process's completion time.

---

## Step 3: Find Completion Time

The time at which a process's execution finishes is its **Completion Time (CT)**.

From:

```text
0       5       8       10
|-------|-------|--------|
   P1      P2       P3
```

we get:

```text
P1 → CT = 5
P2 → CT = 8
P3 → CT = 10
```

---

## Step 4: Calculate Turnaround Time

Use:

$$
\boxed{TAT = CT - AT}
$$

For each process:

```text
P1 → CT - AT
P2 → CT - AT
P3 → CT - AT
```

---

## Step 5: Calculate Waiting Time

Use:

$$
\boxed{WT = TAT - BT}
$$

or equivalently:

$$
\boxed{WT = CT - AT - BT}
$$

---

# Complete Multi-Process Example

Suppose three processes arrive at time 0:

| Process | AT | BT |
| ------- | -: | -: |
| P1      |  0 |  5 |
| P2      |  0 |  3 |
| P3      |  0 |  2 |

Assume **FCFS**.

Therefore the execution order is:

```text
P1 → P2 → P3
```

### Gantt Chart

```text
Time →    0         5       8      10
          |---------|-------|-------|
             P1        P2      P3
```

Therefore:

```text
P1 → CT = 5
P2 → CT = 8
P3 → CT = 10
```

Now calculate TAT and WT.

### P1

$$
TAT = CT - AT
$$

$$
= 5 - 0 = 5
$$

$$
WT = TAT - BT
$$

$$
= 5 - 5 = 0
$$

---

### P2

$$
TAT = 8 - 0 = 8
$$

$$
WT = 8 - 3 = 5
$$

---

### P3

$$
TAT = 10 - 0 = 10
$$

$$
WT = 10 - 2 = 8
$$

### Final Table

| Process | AT | BT | CT | TAT = CT − AT | WT = TAT − BT |
| ------- | -: | -: | -: | ------------: | ------------: |
| P1      |  0 |  5 |  5 |             5 |             0 |
| P2      |  0 |  3 |  8 |             8 |             5 |
| P3      |  0 |  2 | 10 |            10 |             8 |

---

# Average Waiting Time

When multiple processes are involved, we can calculate **Average Waiting Time**.

### Formula

$$
\boxed{\text{Average WT} = \frac{\sum WT}{n}}
$$

For the above example:

```text
WT(P1) = 0
WT(P2) = 5
WT(P3) = 8
```

Therefore:

$$
\text{Average WT}
=
\frac{0+5+8}{3}
$$

$$
=
\frac{13}{3}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Average WT} \approx 4.33}
$$

---

# Average Turnaround Time

Similarly:

$$
\boxed{\text{Average TAT} = \frac{\sum TAT}{n}}
$$

For our example:

```text
TAT(P1) = 5
TAT(P2) = 8
TAT(P3) = 10
```

Therefore:

$$
\text{Average TAT}
=
\frac{5+8+10}{3}
$$

$$
=
\frac{23}{3}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Average TAT} \approx 7.67}
$$

---

# Waiting Time Is Not the Same as Turnaround Time

This distinction is important.

### Turnaround Time

Measures:

```text
Arrival → Completion
```

It includes:

* Waiting
* CPU execution
* Other time spent in the system

### Waiting Time

Measures the time spent waiting for CPU in the ready queue.

```text
Waiting Time
     ↓
Ready Queue → CPU
```

Therefore:

```text
Turnaround Time ≥ Waiting Time
```

for the simple CPU-burst model, because turnaround includes the actual CPU execution time.

---

# Important Note About I/O and Multiple CPU Bursts

The formula:

$$
WT = TAT - BT
$$

works when **BT represents the total CPU burst time** and the standard scheduling problem treats the remaining time as waiting/system time in the expected way.

In more detailed systems, a process may have:

```text
CPU → I/O → CPU → I/O → CPU
```

For example:

```text
P1:

CPU
 ↓
I/O
 ↓
CPU
 ↓
I/O
 ↓
CPU
 ↓
Completion
```

In such cases, turnaround time can include:

* CPU execution time
* Ready-queue waiting time
* I/O waiting/blocking time

Therefore, if the question specifically asks for **ready-queue waiting time**, be careful not to interpret every non-CPU interval as CPU waiting.

For standard OS scheduling problems, however, the commonly used formula remains:

$$
\boxed{WT = TAT - BT}
$$

where **BT is the total CPU burst time**.

---

# Waiting Time in Preemptive Scheduling

In preemptive scheduling, a process can be interrupted multiple times.

For example:

```text
P1 → CPU
     ↓
   preempted
     ↓
P2 → CPU
     ↓
P1 → CPU again
```

P1's waiting time may consist of **multiple separate intervals** in the ready queue.

For example:

```text
P1:
Running → Waiting → Running → Waiting → Running
```

All ready-queue waiting intervals must be considered when determining its total waiting time.

The formula:

$$
WT = TAT - BT
$$

still provides the total ready-queue waiting time under the standard CPU-burst model.

---

# Example of Preemption

Suppose:

```text
P1:
Arrival = 0
Burst   = 8

P2:
Arrival = 2
Burst   = 3
```

Suppose a preemptive scheduler interrupts P1 when P2 arrives:

```text
Time →    0       2       5          11
          |-------|-------|-----------|
             P1      P2        P1
```

P1:

```text
Runs:     0 → 2
Waits:    2 → 5
Runs:     5 → 11
```

P1's total CPU burst is:

```text
2 + 6 = 8
```

Its waiting time is:

```text
5 - 2 = 3
```

Its turnaround time is:

```text
11 - 0 = 11
```

Therefore:

$$
TAT = 11
$$

and:

$$
WT = 11 - 8 = 3
$$

This demonstrates why a Gantt chart is especially useful for preemptive scheduling problems.

# Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1: Using Burst Time Instead of Completion Time

Incorrect:

```text
TAT = BT - AT
```

Correct:

$$
\boxed{TAT = CT - AT}
$$

---

## Mistake 2: Confusing Waiting Time With Turnaround Time

Incorrect:

```text
WT = CT - AT
```

That is actually:

```text
TAT = CT - AT
```

Correct:

$$
\boxed{WT = TAT - BT}
$$

---

## Mistake 3: Forgetting Arrival Time

If processes arrive at different times, you must use their individual arrival times.

For example:

```text
P1 → AT = 0
P2 → AT = 5
```

Do not assume every process starts counting from time 0.

---

## Mistake 4: Calculating From the Wrong Gantt Chart

The scheduling algorithm determines the execution order.

Therefore, first solve:

```text
Scheduling algorithm
        ↓
Execution order
        ↓
Gantt chart
        ↓
Completion Time
        ↓
Turnaround Time
        ↓
Waiting Time
```

# Memory Trick

Remember the flow:

```text
Arrival
   ↓
Waiting
   ↓
CPU Burst
   ↓
Completion
```

Therefore:

```text
Turnaround = Arrival → Completion

Waiting = Turnaround − CPU Burst
```

Or simply:

$$
\boxed{TAT = CT - AT}
$$

$$
\boxed{WT = TAT - BT}
$$

$$
\boxed{WT = CT - AT - BT}
$$

# Interview Takeaway

> **Turnaround Time measures the total time from a process's arrival until its completion, while Waiting Time measures how much time the process spends waiting for CPU time in the ready queue.**

The key formulas are:

$$
\boxed{TAT = CT - AT}
$$

$$
\boxed{WT = TAT - BT}
$$

and therefore:

$$
\boxed{WT = CT - AT - BT}
$$

For multiple processes:

$$
\boxed{\text{Average WT} = \frac{\sum WT}{n}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Average TAT} = \frac{\sum TAT}{n}}
$$

### One-line memory trick

```text
TAT = "How long was the process in the system?"

WT  = "How long did the process wait for CPU?"
```
