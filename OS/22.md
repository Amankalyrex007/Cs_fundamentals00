22.What is deadlock?
A deadlock is a situation in which two or more processes/threads are permanently waiting for resources held by each other, so none of them can continue.

Simple example

Suppose there are two threads and two locks:

Thread 1:
    holds Lock A
    waits for Lock B

Thread 2:
    holds Lock B
    waits for Lock A

Neither thread can proceed:

Thread 1 → holds A → waiting for B
                         ↑
                         │
Thread 2 → holds B ──────┘

This creates a circular wait, resulting in deadlock.

Four necessary conditions

A deadlock can occur when all four of these conditions exist simultaneously:

Mutual exclusion — a resource can be held by only one process/thread at a time.
Hold and wait — a process holds one resource while waiting for another.
No preemption — a resource cannot simply be forcibly taken away.
Circular wait — a circular chain exists where each process waits for a resource held by the next process.

These are known as the Coffman conditions.

How can deadlock be prevented or handled?

Common approaches include:

Consistent lock ordering — always acquire multiple locks in the same order.
Avoid hold-and-wait — acquire all required resources together when practical.
Timeouts — stop waiting indefinitely for a lock.
Deadlock detection — detect cycles and recover.
Rollback/termination — release resources by aborting or rolling back a selected process/thread.
Deadlock vs starvation
Deadlock	Starvation
Processes wait for each other in a cycle	A process keeps getting denied resources/CPU
No involved process can make progress	Other processes may continue making progress
Often caused by circular resource dependencies	Often caused by unfair scheduling/resource allocation
Interview takeaway

Deadlock occurs when processes or threads become permanently blocked because each is waiting for a resource held by another. The four Coffman conditions are mutual exclusion, hold and wait, no preemption, and circular wait.
